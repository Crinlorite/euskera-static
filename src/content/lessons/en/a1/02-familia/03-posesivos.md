Possessives in Basque are placed **before the noun**, just like in English. They don't change form based on the gender of the possessed object (there is no masculine/feminine version, just *nire*).

## Complete table

| Person | Possessive | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| ni | **nire** | nire ama | my mother |
| zu | **zure** | zure aita | your father |
| hura | **bere** | bere arreba | his/her sister |
| gu | **gure** | gure etxea | our house |
| zuek | **zuen** | zuen lagunak | your friends |
| haiek | **beren** | beren autoa | their car |

> Unlike in English, the noun **keeps the article `-a`** even with a possessive: *gure etxea* (our house), *zuen lagunak* (your friends), *beren autoa* (their car). In words that already end in `-a` (like *ama* or *arreba*), the article isn't noticeable: *nire ama*.

## Family + possessives

Combine what you've learned to describe your family:

- *Nire aita Mattin da.* — My father is Mattin.
- *Zure ama nongoa da?* — Where is your mother from?
- *Bere arreba Donostiakoa da.* — His/Her sister is from Donostia.
- *Gure aiton-amonak Bilbon bizi dira.* — Our grandparents live in Bilbao.
