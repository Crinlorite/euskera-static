Beyond parents, siblings, and children, the family extends to uncles, cousins, nephews/nieces, and in-laws. Here is the most common vocabulary.

## Uncles and cousins

| Euskera | English |
|---|---|
| *osaba* | uncle |
| *izeba* | aunt |
| *lehengusu* | male cousin |
| *lehengusina* | female cousin |
| *iloba* | nephew/niece (also "grandchild" in some dialects — the context decides) |

## In-laws

| Euskera | English |
|---|---|
| *aitaginarreba* | father-in-law |
| *amaginarreba* | mother-in-law |
| *koinatu* | brother-in-law |
| *koinata* | sister-in-law |
| *errain* | daughter-in-law |
| *suhi* | son-in-law |

## Usage patterns

- *Nire osaba Bilbon bizi da.* — My uncle lives in Bilbao.
- *Bere lehengusinak hamabi urte ditu.* — His/Her female cousin is twelve years old.
- *Zure koinata euskalduna da?* — Is your sister-in-law Basque?

> The words for in-laws are often formed by combining existing words (*aita-* + *ginarreba*). You don't need to memorize the internal logic; they'll stick with use.
