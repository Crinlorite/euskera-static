In standard Basque (*euskara batua*) there are seven personal pronouns:

| Pronoun | Who | Verb "izan" (NOR) |
|---|---|---|
| **ni** | I | naiz |
| **hi** | you (informal/close) | haiz |
| **zu** | you (standard) | zara |
| **hura** / **bera** | he, she | da |
| **gu** | we | gara |
| **zuek** | you (plural) | zarete |
| **haiek** | they | dira |

> There is no gender distinction: *hura* works for he and she; *haiek* for they. **Bera** is a very common variant of *hura* in everyday speech.

## Hi vs zu (important)

In Basque, there are two forms of "you":

- **zu** → standard you. It's what you use in class, with strangers, with coworkers, with everyone in any neutral context. **In A1, always use `zu`.**
- **hi** → intimate you (*hitano*). Only used between close friends, family, or in some towns as the usual form. It has its own conjugation (`haiz`, `duk`/`dun` with a marker for the listener's gender). We don't cover it in A1.

If in doubt, **use `zu`** — it always works.

## Negation

To negate, you place **ez** before the verb:

- *Ni ez naiz Egoitz.* — I am not Egoitz.
- *Hau ez da nire kalea.* — This is not my street.
- *Gu ez gara ikasleak.* — We are not students.

## Complete pattern

- **Ni** ikaslea **naiz**. — I am a student.
- **Zu** irakaslea **zara**. — You are a teacher.
- **Hura** medikua **da**. — He/she is a doctor.
- **Gu** lagunak **gara**. — We are friends.
- **Zuek** anaiak **zarete**. — You (plural) are brothers.
- **Haiek** umeak **dira**. — They are children.
