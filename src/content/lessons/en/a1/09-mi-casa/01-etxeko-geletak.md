Let's start with the most basic part of any home: the **rooms**. The general word for "room / hall" is **gela**, and most names for rooms are formed by combining a root with `-gela`.

## Basic Vocabulary

| Basque | English |
|---|---|
| *etxea* | the house |
| *pisua* | the flat / apartment (a home in a building) |
| *gela* | room / hall |
| *sukaldea* | kitchen |
| *logela* | bedroom |
| *komuna* | bathroom |
| *egongela* | living room |
| *sarrera* | entrance |
| *korridorea* / *pasabidea* | hallway / corridor |
| *balkoia* | balcony |
| *terraza* | terrace |

## How some names are formed

Basque is very transparent with these words. If you look at how they're composed, you'll remember them better:

- **logela** → *lo* (sleep) + *gela* (room) = "sleep room" → bedroom
- **egongela** → *egon* (to be) + *gela* = "being room" → living room
- **sukaldea** → *su* (fire) + *aldea* (area) = "fire area" → kitchen

> **Komuna** and **bainugela** are synonyms: *komuna* is the everyday word, while *bainugela* (literally "bathroom") is a bit more formal.

## Building: floor and elevator

| Basque | English |
|---|---|
| *solairua* | floor (1st, 2nd…) |
| *igogailua* | elevator / lift |
| *eskailera* | stairs |
| *ataria* | entrance hall / lobby |

To say which floor you live on:

- *Hirugarren solairuan bizi naiz.* — I live on the third floor.
- *Etxeak igogailua du.* — The house has an elevator.

## Useful Questions

- **Zenbat gela ditu zure etxeak?** — How many rooms does your house have?
- **Zenbat logela ditu?** — How many bedrooms does it have?
- **Non dago sukaldea?** — Where is the kitchen?
- **Etxeak badu balkoirik?** — Does the house have a balcony?

> **Cultural note:** in Navarre and the Basque-Castilian area, it's common to say *pisua* for an urban dwelling and *etxea* for a family/village house. In daily life, they are used interchangeably without issue.
