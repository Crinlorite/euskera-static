A busy schedule is full of appointments and plans. This lesson teaches you how to talk about them: having an appointment, meeting up with someone, saying if you're free.

## Key vocabulary

| Basque | English |
|---|---|
| *hitzordua* | appointment (an arranged time with someone) |
| *bilera* | meeting |
| *errebisioa* | check-up (medical, dental) |
| *medikua* | the doctor |
| *dentista* | dentist |
| *kontsulta* | consultation |

## Having an appointment or meeting

To say "I have X," you use the verb *ukan* (`dut, du, dugu, ...`):

- **Bilera dut.** — I have a meeting.
- **Hitzordua dut medikuarekin.** — I have an appointment with the doctor.
- **Errebisioa du semearekin.** — He/She has a check-up with his/her son.

> If you want to specify the time, you just add it at the beginning or the end: *Bostetan bilera dut* / *Bilera dut bostetan*.

## Meeting up with someone — `geratu` + `-ekin`

`Geratu` means "to meet up" or "to stay," and it's combined with the sociative suffix `-ekin` (with):

| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| *X-ekin geratu naiz* | *Egoitz**ekin** geratu naiz* | I met up with Egoitz |
| *X-ekin geratuko naiz* | *Egoitz**ekin** geratuko naiz* | I will meet up with Egoitz |

> The suffix `-ekin` attaches to the noun. If it ends in a consonant, you add *-ekin*: *Mikel + ekin = Mikelekin*, *Maialenekin*. If it ends in a vowel, you add *-rekin*: *Garazirekin, Anerekin*.

## Arranging the time

Typical phrases to set a time:

- **Zer ordutan geratuko gara?** — What time shall we meet?
- **Bostetan ondo?** — Is five o'clock good?
- **Sei eta erdietan geratuko gara.** — We'll meet at half past six.
- **Bederatziak aldera.** — Around nine.
- **Hamabietan.** — At twelve.

`Aldera` = "around," "about." Very useful when you don't want to commit to an exact time.

## Accepting and declining

| Yes | No / excuse |
|---|---|
| *Bai, ondo!* | *Ezin dut, beste plan bat dut.* |
| *Ados!* | *Ezin dut, lanpetuta nago.* |
| *Ederki!* | *Ezin dut, beste afari bat dudalako.* |
| *Konforme!* | *Beste egun batean, mesedez.* |

## Being free / busy

- **Libre nago.** — I'm free.
- **Ordu hori libre dut.** — I have that time free.
- **Lanpetuta nago.** — I'm busy.
- **Gauza asko egin behar ditut.** — I have a lot of things to do.

## Mini-dialogue

> — *Aizu, kafe bat hartuko dugu bihar?*
>
> — *Bai, oso ondo. Zer ordutan geratuko gara?*
>
> — *Bostak aldera ondo?*
>
> — *Ezin dut bostetan, dentista dut. Sei eta erdietan?*
>
> — *Ederki, sei eta erdietan tabernan.*

## Making excuses with `-elako`

When you decline something, it's natural to give the reason using the suffix **-elako** (because):

- *Ezin dut, lan egin behar du**dalako**.* — I can't, **because** I have to work.
- *Ezin dut, gaixorik nago**elako**.* — I can't, **because** I'm sick.
- *Ezin dut, amarekin egon behar du**dalako**.* — I can't, **because** I have to be with my mother.

> **Cultural note:** in *euskaltegis* (Basque language schools) and formal settings, *hitzordua* is reserved for specific appointments (doctor, hairdresser). Among friends, it's more natural to use *geratu* + *-ekin*, without the word *hitzordua* unless you want to emphasize that it's something formal.
