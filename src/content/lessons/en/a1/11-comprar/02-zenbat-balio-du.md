To go shopping, you need to know how to ask for the price and understand the answer. In Basque, there are two basic questions, depending on whether you're looking at a specific product on the counter or you're ready to pay.

## The two key questions

| Basque | English | When |
|---|---|---|
| *Zenbat balio du?* | How much does it cost? | looking at a specific product |
| *Zenbat da?* | How much is it? | at the end, when paying |
| *Zenbatean dago kiloa?* | How much is a kilo? | asking the price per unit |

`Zenbat` means "how much/how many". *Balio* is "to be worth". *Du* is the "has/does" form of the verb *ukan*.

## Responding with the price

There are two common structures:

- **X euro balio du** — It costs X euros.
   - *Botilak lau euro balio du.* → The bottle costs 4 euros.
- **Kiloa X eurotan dago** — The kilo is X euros.
   - *Kiloa hamar eurotan dago.* → The kilo is 10 euros.

> *Eurotan* is "in euros" (plural inessive). In daily speech, it's also very common to just hear "*Hamar euro*" without a verb, just like in English.

## If you find it expensive or cheap

- **Garestia da** — It's expensive.
- **Merkea da** — It's cheap.
- **Hau garestia da!** — This is expensive!
- **Merkeak ere ez!** — They aren't cheap either! (literally: "not even cheap")

## Paying and the change

- **Zenbat da dena?** — How much is everything?
- **Tori!** — Here you go! (when giving the money, very common)
- **Txartelaz ordain dezaket?** — Can I pay by card?
- **Hemen daukazu kanbioa.** — Here's your change.

> **Cultural note:** in many small neighborhood shops, paying in cash is still the norm, so *txartelaz ordain dezaket?* is a useful question when you go in for the first time.
