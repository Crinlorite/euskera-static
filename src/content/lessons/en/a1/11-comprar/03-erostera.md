This lesson teaches the phrases that are repeated over and over again in a shop. With four or five formulas, you can handle almost any situation.

## The Turn-Taking Ritual

In small shops, people take turns. The basic formula upon entering is:

- **Azkena, mesedez?** — Who's last, please?

When it's your turn, they'll say:

- **Zer nahi duzu?** — What do you want?
- **Esan!** — Tell me!

## Asking for What You Want

Three common ways, from more colloquial to more polite:

| Basque | English | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| **Eman kilo bat sagar, mesedez.** | Give me a kilo of apples, please. | direct, neutral |
| **Jarriko al didazu kilo bat sagar?** | Can you give me a kilo of apples? | polite (future tense + politeness) |
| **Hartuko dut kilo bat sagar.** | I'll take a kilo of apples. | confirming a choice |

> *-Ko / -go* is the future tense marker: *jarri → jarriko*, *hartu → hartuko*. It's very commonly used to soften requests.

## The Shopkeeper's Questions

- **Nolakoa nahi duzu?** — What kind do you want? (what quality / type)
- **Zenbat nahi duzu?** — How much do you want?
- **Besterik?** / **Besterik nahi duzu?** — Anything else?
- **Pentsatu duzu?** — Have you decided?

And to finish:

- **Ez, besterik ez. Eskerrik asko.** — No, nothing else. Thank you very much.

## Finalizing the Purchase

- **Zenbat da dena?** — How much is it all together?
- **Tori!** — Here you go! (when handing over money)
- **Eskerrik asko! Agur!** — Thank you! Goodbye!
- **Zuri! Agur!** — You're welcome! Goodbye! (the shopkeeper's reply)

> **Cultural note:** *Tori* is a short word that's used a lot when passing something from hand to hand: money, a bag, a key. It's very useful to learn it early on.
