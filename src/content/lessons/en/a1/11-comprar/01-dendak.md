When you go shopping in Euskal Herria, each product usually has its own specialized shop, especially in towns and neighborhoods. Learning the names helps you get around and also understand the signs.

## Food shops

| Basque | English | What is sold |
|---|---|---|
| *harategia* | butcher's shop | haragia (meat) |
| *okindegia* | bakery | ogia (bread) |
| *arrandegia* | fishmonger's | arraina (fish) |
| *fruta-denda* | greengrocer's | frutak, barazkiak |
| *supermerkatua* | supermarket | everything |
| *hipermerkatua* | hypermarket | large stores |
| *azoka* / *plaza* | market / square | various stalls |

## The hidden clue: *-degi*

Many shop names end in **-degi**, an old suffix that means "place where there is…":

- *ogi* (bread) → **okindegia** (bakery)
- *haragi* (meat) → **harategia** (butcher's shop)
- *arrain* (fish) → **arrandegia** (fishmonger's)
- *gozo* (sweet) → **gozotegia** (pastry shop)

> Once you discover the *-degi* pattern, you'll be able to recognize other shops even if you haven't seen them before.

## Saying where you're going

To go to a shop, use the **NORA** case with the suffix `-ra` (destination):

- *Okindegi**ra** noa.* — I'm going to the bakery.
- *Harategi**ra** joango naiz.* — I will go to the butcher's shop.
- *Fruta-denda**ra** joaten naiz astero.* — I go to the greengrocer's every week.

> **Cultural note:** many families still do their shopping separately: fish at the neighborhood *arrandegia*, bread at the *okindegia*, and only the rest at the supermarket. It's common to hear "*okindegira noa*" as a routine expression.
