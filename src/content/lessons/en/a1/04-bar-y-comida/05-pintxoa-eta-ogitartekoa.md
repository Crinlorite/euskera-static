The great Basque bar culture revolves around three main formats: the **pintxo** (small, served at the bar), the **ogitarteko** (sandwich), and the **plater konbinatua** (combo plate). Knowing how to order variations allows you to eat anywhere.

## The three formats

| Basque | English | Size |
|---|---|---|
| *pintxoa* | pintxo / small tapa | small, served at the bar |
| *ogitartekoa* | sandwich | medium, on bread |
| *plater konbinatua* | combo plate | full meal |
| *razioa* | portion / serving | to share |

> **Ogitarteko** literally means "the thing between breads": *ogi* (bread) + *arte* (between) + *-ko*. It's the classic sandwich, but made with good Basque bread and generous fillings.

## Typical fillings

These are the most common fillings you'll see on a bar's blackboard:

| Basque | English |
|---|---|
| *tortilla* | Spanish omelette |
| *txistorra* | txistorra |
| *txorizoa* | chorizo |
| *gazta* | cheese |
| *urdaiazpikoa* | ham |
| *antxoa* | anchovy |
| *atuna* | tuna |
| *gilda* | gilda (anchovy + chili pepper + olive) |

## How to combine to order

The formula is: **descriptor + format + bat (mesedez)**. The descriptor (the filling) goes first.

- *Tortilla **pintxo bat**, mesedez.* — A tortilla pintxo, please.
- *Gazta **ogitartekoa**, mesedez.* — A cheese sandwich, please.
- *Txistorra **razio bat** hartuko dugu.* — We'll have a portion of txistorra.
- *Ogitarteko **mistoa**, mesedez.* — A ham and cheese sandwich, please.

> When you order to share, you use **dugu** ("we have / we're having") or **hartuko dugu** ("we will have"). In a group, it's very common to order several portions for everyone.

## Variations and nuances

- **handia** — large / **txikia** — small:
  - *Garagardo handia* — large beer
  - *Kafe txikia* — small coffee (small cup)
- **hotza** — cold / **beroa** — hot:
  - *Ogitarteko beroa* — hot sandwich (grilled)
- **eta...** — and... (to add something):
  - *Tortilla pintxoa **eta** ardo gorri bat* — a tortilla pintxo and a red wine

## A table of friends

> — *Zer hartuko dugu?* — What are we having?
>
> — *Niretzat tortilla pintxoa eta zurito bat.* — For me, a tortilla pintxo and a small beer.
>
> — *Eta zuretzat?* — And for you?
>
> — *Nik gazta razio bat eta ardo gorri bat hartuko ditut.* — I'll have a portion of cheese and a red wine.
>
> — *Nik plater konbinatu bat: arrautzak, txorizoa eta patata frijituak.* — I'll have a combo plate: eggs, chorizo, and french fries.
>
> — *Bale, hiru eskaera dira.* — OK, that's three orders.

## Paying

- **Pagatzera!** — Time to pay!
- **Niri tokatzen zait.** — It's my turn.
- **Nik gonbidatzen zaitut.** — I'm treating you.
- **Erdibana.** — Let's go halves / fifty-fifty. (to share)

> **Cultural note:** pintxos are an especially strong tradition in San Sebastián, Bilbao, and Pamplona. Each bar usually has a house specialty. The custom of **poteo** consists of going from bar to bar having a pintxo and a drink in each one — a very social and very Basque tradition.
