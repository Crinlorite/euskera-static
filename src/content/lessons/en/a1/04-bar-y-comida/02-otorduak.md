Each meal of the day has its name in Basque, and knowing them will help you talk about your daily routine. There is also a specifically Basque meal that has no direct equivalent in English: the **hamaiketako**.

## The five meals of the day

| Basque | English | When |
|---|---|---|
| *gosaria* | breakfast | morning |
| *hamaiketakoa* | mid-morning snack | around 11 a.m. |
| *bazkaria* | lunch (the main meal) | 1-2 p.m. |
| *askaria* | afternoon snack | afternoon |
| *afaria* | dinner | evening |

> **Hamaiketakoa** literally means "the one from eleven" (*hamaika* = eleven). It's the typical Basque mid-morning break: a *pintxo*, a coffee, or a *zurito* with something. It's not a big breakfast or a full lunch; it's something in between that you have out, at a tavern, with coworkers or friends.

## Verbs: jan, edan and the specific ones

| Basque | English |
|---|---|
| *jan* | to eat (general) |
| *edan* | to drink |
| *gosaldu* | to have breakfast |
| *bazkaldu* | to have lunch |
| *afaldu* | to have dinner |

> There are specific verbs for each meal (just like English has "to have breakfast / to have lunch / to have dinner"). At the A1 level, you will recognize them more than you will conjugate them — for now, it's enough to know they exist.

## Hunger and thirst

To say "I'm hungry" / "I'm thirsty" in Basque, you **do not use the verb "to have"** (like in Spanish or French), but rather the verb **izan** ("to be"):

- **Gose naiz.** — I'm hungry. (literally: "I am hungry")
- **Egarri naiz.** — I'm thirsty. (literally: "I am thirsty")
- **Maialen gose da.** — Maialen is hungry.
- **Gu egarri gara.** — We are thirsty.

> This construction might surprise you. In Basque, physical sensations like hunger, thirst, and sleepiness are often expressed with *izan* + adjective, as if they were a state of being. You'll also see it with *logura naiz* (I'm sleepy). Be careful: "I'm cold" doesn't follow this pattern — it's said *hotzak nago*, with *egon*.

## Typical phrases

- *Goizean gosaria hartzen dut.* — In the morning I have breakfast (literally: "I take the breakfast").
- *Hamaiketakoa egiten dugu lankideekin.* — We have the *hamaiketako* with coworkers.
- *Etxean bazkaltzen dut.* — I have lunch at home.
- *Afari ona prestatu dugu.* — We have prepared a good dinner.
- *Gose naiz, zer dago jateko?* — I'm hungry, what is there to eat?

> **Cultural note:** meal times in the Basque Country are usually similar to the rest of the peninsula: early breakfast, *hamaiketako* around eleven, *bazkaria* around 2-3 p.m., afternoon snack around 6 p.m., dinner at 9 p.m. or later.
