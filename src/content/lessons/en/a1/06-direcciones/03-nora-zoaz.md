The **NORA case** (also called *adlative*) marks the **destination** of a movement: where you are going. In English, it's usually translated with the preposition **"to"** ("I'm going *to* the house", "*to* Bilbao", "*to the* park").

## The question: Nora?

| Question | English |
|---|---|
| ***Nora* zoaz?** | Where are you going? |
| ***Nora* doa?** | Where is he/she/it going? |
| ***Nora* goaz?** | Where are we going? |
| ***Nora* doaz?** | Where are they going? |

## The suffix: -ra

To answer, we add **`-ra`** to the destination. The pattern adapts depending on how the word ends:

| Ending of the place | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -a (organic, *eskola*) | + ra | eskola → **eskolara** |
| Vowel without a final -a (*Bilbo*, *etxe*) | + ra | etxe → **etxera**, Bilbo → **Bilbora** |
| Consonant (*Madril*, *Gasteiz*) | + era | Madril → **Madrilera**, Gasteiz → **Gasteizera** |

## Table of examples

| Place | -ra form | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| etxe (house) | **etxera** | *Etxera noa.* — I'm going home. |
| eskola | **eskolara** | *Eskolara doaz.* — They are going to school. |
| euskaltegi | **euskaltegira** | *Euskaltegira goaz.* — We are going to the euskaltegi. |
| taberna | **tabernara** | *Tabernara zoaz?* — Are you going to the bar? |
| lan (work) | **lanera** | *Lanera nator.* — I'm coming to work. (also -era after a consonant) |
| Bilbo | **Bilbora** | *Bilbora doa.* — He/She is going to Bilbao. |
| Donostia | **Donostiara** | *Donostiara goaz asteburuan.* — We're going to Donostia on the weekend. |
| Madril | **Madrilera** | *Madrilera doaz.* — They are going to Madrid. |
| mendi (mountain) | **mendira** | *Mendira goaz larunbatean.* — We're going to the mountain on Saturday. |

> **Quick tip:** *non* → *nora* follow the same pattern as *kale-an* → *kale-ra*. If you know how to form *Bilbon* (in Bilbao), you already know how to form *Bilbora* (to Bilbao): just change *-n* to *-ra*. If you know how to form *Madrilen*, you know how to form *Madrilera*.

## Zertara? — "What for?"

To ask **what** you are going for (with what purpose), you use **Zertara zoaz?** and answer with a verb + the suffix **`-tzera`**:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| *Zertara zoaz tabernara?* | *Kafea hartzera.* — To have a coffee. |
| *Zertara doa Kepa supermerkatura?* | *Erostera.* — To buy/shop. |
| *Zertara goaz mendira?* | *Ibiltzera.* — To walk. |
| *Zertara doaz hondartzara?* | *Igeri egitera.* — To swim. |

> **Pattern:** *(verb in participle form) + -t(z)era*: *erosi → erostera*, *jan → jatera*, *ikasi → ikastera*, *ikusi → ikustera*, *bisitatu → bisitatzera*. It's basically the "to + infinitive" in English.

## Mini-conversation

> — *Nora zoaz, Maialen?* — Where are you going, Maialen?
>
> — *Euskaltegira noa.* — I'm going to the euskaltegi.
>
> — *Eta zertara?* — And what for?
>
> — *Klasera, noski! Euskara ikastera.* — To class, of course! To learn Basque.
>
> — *Eta gero?* — And afterwards?
>
> — *Tabernara goaz lagunekin pote bat hartzera.* — We're going to the bar with friends to have a drink.

## Directional adverbs

Demonstratives also have their NORA form:

| NON (locative) | NORA (direction) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| hemen | **hona** | to here |
| hor | **horra** | to there (near you) |
| han | **hara** | to there (over there) |

- **Hona zatoz?** — Are you coming here?
- **Hara doaz.** — They are going over there.

> **Important reminder:** this confuses many people at first: *etxean* (at home) ≠ *etxera* (to home) ≠ *etxetik* (from home, lesson 04). The three look similar because they all talk about "home", but the information is completely different. Make a mental note: **NON = -an (to be somewhere)**, **NORA = -ra (to go somewhere)**, **NONDIK = -tik (to come from somewhere, lesson 4)**.
