Three basic verbs will come up all the time: **joan** (to go), **etorri** (to come), and **ibili** (to walk, "to move from one place to another"). All three have **synthetic** forms (without an auxiliary verb) that are used very frequently in the present tense — and it's a good idea to learn them as a block.

## Joan — to go

| Person | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **noa** | I go |
| zu | **zoaz** | you go |
| hura | **doa** | he/she/it goes |
| gu | **goaz** | we go |
| zuek | **zoazte** | you (pl.) go |
| haiek | **doaz** | they go |

> The key question: ***Nora zoaz?*** — Where are you going? We'll work on this in lesson 03.

## Etorri — to come

| Person | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **nator** | I come |
| zu | **zatoz** | you come |
| hura | **dator** | he/she/it comes |
| gu | **gatoz** | we come |
| zuek | **zatozte** | you (pl.) come |
| haiek | **datoz** | they come |

> The key question: ***Nondik zatoz?*** — Where do you come from? We'll work on this in lesson 04.

## Ibili — to walk, to move around

| Person | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **nabil** | I walk, I'm moving around |
| zu | **zabiltza** | you walk |
| hura | **dabil** | he/she/it walks |
| gu | **gabiltza** | we walk |
| zuek | **zabiltzate** | you (pl.) walk |
| haiek | **dabiltza** | they walk |

> *Ibili* is also used to ask "what are you up to": **Zer zabiltza?** — How's it going? / What are you up to? It's a very common greeting between people who know each other.

## Other useful verbs of movement

| Basque | English | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *sartu* | to enter | *Tabernan sartu naiz.* — I have entered the bar. |
| *irten* | to leave, to go out | *Etxetik irten naiz.* — I have left the house. |
| *igo* | to go up, to climb | *Eskailerak igo ditut.* — I have gone up the stairs. |
| *jaitsi* | to go down | *Aldapa jaitsi dut.* — I have gone down the slope. |
| *iritsi / heldu* | to arrive | *Iruñera iritsi naiz.* — I have arrived in Pamplona. |

## Why synthetic forms matter

In Basque, most verbs are conjugated with an **auxiliary** verb: *jan dut* (I have eaten), *ikusi dut* (I have seen). But a few very common verbs — among them **joan**, **etorri**, **egon**, **ibili**, **eduki**, **jakin** — have their own "compact" (*synthetic*) conjugation that is used in the present tense. Since they appear so often, it's easier to memorize the six forms than to use roundabout constructions.

## Mini-conversation

> — *Aupa, nora zoaz?* — Hey! Where are you going?
>
> — *Banketxera **noa**. Eta zu, nondik **zatoz**?* — I'm going to the bank. And you, where are you coming from?
>
> — *Lanetik **nator**.* — I'm coming from work.
>
> — *Eta Lide? Zer **dabil**?* — And Lide? What is she up to?
>
> — *Enkargutan **dabil**, batetik bestera.* — She's running errands, from one place to another.

> **Note:** the synthetic forms are a central building block of A1. Memorize them in blocks of 2 (ni/zu) or 3 (gu/zuek/haiek) by singing, writing, and repeating. The effort will pay off with practice.
