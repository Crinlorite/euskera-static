To wrap up the unit, we're missing one piece: **how you get around**. In English, we use "by + transport" ("by car", "by bus"). In Basque, the suffix **`-z`** (*instrumental* case) is used. It's added to the means of transport and, surprisingly, also to the word for "foot".

## The question

| Question | English |
|---|---|
| ***Nola* zoaz?** | How are you going? |
| ***Zerez* zoaz?** | By what (means) are you going? |
| ***Nola* doa Iruñera?** | How is he/she going to Pamplona? |

Both forms (*Nola* and *Zerez*) are valid and common. *Nola* is more general; *zerez* directly implies "by what means".

## Means of transport

| Means | Form with -z | English |
|---|---|---|
| oin (foot) | **oinez** | on foot |
| kotxe / auto | **kotxez** / **autoz** | by car |
| autobus | **autobusez** | by bus |
| tren | **trenez** | by train |
| metro | **metroz** | by metro |
| taxi | **taxiz** | by taxi |
| bizikleta | **bizikletaz** | by bicycle |
| motor | **motorrez** | by motorcycle |
| hegazkin | **hegazkinez** | by plane |
| itsasontzi | **itsasontziz** | by boat |
| furgoneta | **furgonetaz** | by van |
| zaldi (horse) | **zaldiz** | on horseback |

> The suffix is **`-z`** after a vowel and **`-ez`** after a consonant (*autobus → autobusez*). In practice, almost all transport nouns end in a vowel, so the basic formula is: **means + z**.

## Complete examples

- **Oinez noa lanera.** — I'm going to work on foot.
- **Iruñera trenez goaz.** — We're going to Pamplona by train.
- **Bilbora hegazkinez doaz.** — They're going to Bilbao by plane.
- **Eskolara bizikletaz dator alaba.** — The daughter comes to school by bicycle.
- **Mendira oinez igotzen gara.** — We go up the mountain on foot.

## Combining NONDIK + NORA + NOLA

A typical travel sentence usually has three parts: **from where + to where + how**.

- **Iruñetik Donostiara autobusez doa.** — He/She is going from Pamplona to Donostia by bus.
- **Bilbotik Madrilera trenez gatoz.** — We are coming from Bilbao to Madrid by train.
- **Etxetik euskaltegira oinez noa.** — I'm going from home to the euskaltegi on foot.

## Mini-conversation

> — *Aupa! Bihar Bilbora **noa**.* — Hi! Tomorrow I'm going to Bilbao.
>
> — *Eta **nola** zoaz?* — And how are you going?
>
> — *Trenez. Oso erosoa da.* — By train. It's very comfortable.
>
> — *Ni autoz noa beti, baina garestia da gasolina.* — I always go by car, but gasoline is expensive.

## About "oinez"

At first glance, *oinez* seems like a special case ("with foot"), but it's actually the exact same pattern as the rest: *oin* (foot) + *ez* (instrumental suffix). Basque treats "going on foot" like any other mode of transport: using the means that takes you there.

## Final review of the NORA / NONDIK / NOLA block

| Question | Suffix | Sample Question | Sample Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nora?** | -ra | Where to? | *Bilbo**ra** noa.* |
| **Nondik?** | -tik | From where? | *Bilbo**tik** nator.* |
| **Nola? / Zerez?** | -z | How? / By what? | *Tren**ez** noa.* |

> **Tip:** these three suffixes together can form almost any travel sentence. For example: *Bilbotik Donostiara autobusez goaz, lagun bat bisitatzera*. — "We're going from Bilbao to Donostia by bus, to visit a friend." If you master these three suffixes + the synthetic forms of *joan* and *etorri*, you can talk about any of your daily travels.
