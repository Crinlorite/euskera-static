So far you've learned to talk about yourself (*Ni X naiz*) and about the person you're talking to (*Zu X zara*). In this lesson, you'll add the missing piece: **talking about someone else**, introducing them, saying who they are and what they do.

## Demonstratives

In Basque, there are three levels of proximity with three demonstratives. You'll use them constantly to introduce someone.

| Demonstrative | Distance | English |
|---|---|---|
| **hau** | close to me | this |
| **hori** | close to you | that |
| **hura** | far from both of us | that (over there) |

And their plurals:

| Plural | English |
|---|---|
| **hauek** | these |
| **horiek** | those |
| **haiek** | those (over there) |

## Introducing someone with "Hau ... da"

The basic formula is:

> **Hau** + name/relationship + **da**.

| Sentence | English |
|---|---|
| *Hau Maialen da.* | This is Maialen. |
| *Hau nire laguna da.* | This is my friend. |
| *Hau nire bizilaguna da.* | This is my neighbor. |
| *Hauek nire lankideak dira.* | These are my coworkers. |

> Note: for a single person, you use **da**, and for several, **dira**. It's the same conjugation of *izan* you already know, in the third person.

## Hura and bera

When you have already introduced someone, or when you're talking about a person who isn't present, you use **hura** (standard form) or **bera** (colloquial form, much more frequent).

| Sentence | English |
|---|---|
| *Bera Maialen da.* | She is Maialen. |
| *Bera nire bizilaguna da.* | He/she is my neighbor. |
| *Bera medikua da.* | He/she is a doctor. |

> In Basque, **there is no gender distinction** in pronouns: *bera* works for both "he" and "she". The context or the name makes it clear.

## Putting it all together

Look at this typical introduction, common in class or when first meeting new neighbors:

> — *Kaixo, **hau Maite da**. **Bera** Bilbokoa da, baina orain hemen bizi da. **Bera** kazetaria da.*
>
> — *Pozten naiz zu ezagutzeaz, Maite!*
>
> — *Baita ni ere!*

## Negation: this is NOT...

Just as you already know with *izan*, the negation comes before the verb:

- *Hau **ez da** Egoitz.* — This is not Egoitz.
- *Bera **ez da** mediku, irakaslea da.* — He/she is not a doctor, he/she is a teacher.

> **Tip:** to avoid getting confused with the word order, remember that in a negation, **ez always goes right before the conjugated verb**: *ez da*, *ez naiz*, *ez gara*.
