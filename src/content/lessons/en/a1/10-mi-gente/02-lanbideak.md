## Suffixes that reveal the profession

If you pay attention, many professions have typical suffixes:

- **`-ari` / `-lari`** → "the one who is dedicated to...": *kazetari*, *dantzari*, *mekanikari*, *sukaldari*.
- **`-le`** → "the one who does...": *irakasle*, *idazle*, *ikasle*, *arrantzale*.
- **`-zain` / `-zai`** → "guardian of...": *erizain* (carer of the sick = nurse), *atezaina* (doorman).
- **`-tzaile`** → productive form: *saltzailea* (seller), *suhiltzailea* (firefighter).

Knowing how to identify these suffixes helps you **guess** new professions without a dictionary.

## "To be profession X"

You already know the pattern from the first units:

- **Ni medikua naiz.** — I am a doctor.
- **Bera kazetaria da.** — He/she is a journalist.
- **Gu ikasleak gara.** — We are students.

> Important: to name a profession in general, it takes the article `-a`: *medikua*, *kazetaria*. (In spoken language, you will also hear *mediku naiz*, without the article; both forms are valid, but in this course, we use the form with `-a`.)

## Asking about professions

| Question | English |
|---|---|
| *Zer da zure lanbidea?* | What is your profession? |
| *Zertan egiten duzu lan?* | What do you do for work? |
| *Non egiten duzu lan?* | Where do you work? |
| *Zer da bere lana?* | What is his/her job? |

And to answer about yourself or someone else:

- *Ikaslea naiz, baina dendari moduan ere lan egiten dut.* — I am a student, but I also work as a shop assistant.
- *Bera erizaina da, ospitalean lan egiten du.* — He/she is a nurse, he/she works at the hospital.

> **Note:** in Basque, saying "I am a male nurse" or "I am a female nurse" is said the same way: **erizaina naiz**. There are no gender markers on nouns. You clarify it with context or the person's name.
