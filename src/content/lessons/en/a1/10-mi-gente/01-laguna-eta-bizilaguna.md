In this unit we focus on the people around you: **bizilagunak** (neighbors), **lagunak** (friends), people from work and from class. Basque has precise words to distinguish these types of relationships, and many share the suffix **`-kide`** ("companion/member").

## Key words

| Basque | English | What kind of relationship |
|---|---|---|
| *laguna* | friend | personal trust |
| *lagun mina* | close friend | maximum trust |
| *bizilaguna* | neighbor | shares a building / entrance |
| *auzokidea* | neighbor | from the neighborhood |
| *ikaskidea* | classmate | euskaltegi, high school, university |
| *lankidea* | coworker | from the office |
| *ezaguna* | acquaintance | you know them but not well |
| *ezezaguna* | stranger | you don't know them |

## The -kide suffix

Very productive in Basque. It means "member / companion":

- **ikaskidea** = *ikas-* (to study) + *kide* → study partner
- **lankidea** = *lan* (work) + *kide* → coworker
- **bikotekidea** = *bikote* (couple) + *kide* → partner (in a couple)
- **bizikide** = *bizi* (to live) + *kide* → housemate
- **pisukidea** = *pisu* (apartment/flat) + *kide* → flatmate / roommate

> If you ever can't remember the specific word, *X-kidea* works as an emergency solution: "member/companion of group X".

## Bizilaguna vs auzokidea

Both translate to "neighbor", but there's a nuance:

- **bizilaguna** → someone who lives in your same building / entrance. You see them in the elevator, on the stairs.
- **auzokidea** → a neighbor from the neighborhood, in a broader sense.

In daily conversation they are used interchangeably, but in formal contexts (homeowners' association meetings, notices in the building entrance) **bizilaguna** is usually used.

## Koadrila — more than just a group of friends

The **koadrila** is a social institution very typical of the Basque Country: a close-knit group of friends that usually forms during adolescence and is maintained for decades.

- **Nire koadrilarekin afaltzera noa.** — I'm going to have dinner with my koadrila.
- **Bera nire koadrilakidea da.** — He/She is a member of my koadrila.

> **Cultural note:** the difference between "friends" and a "koadrila" is that the koadrila meets **regularly** (week in, week out), has its own rituals (Sunday lunch, town festivals, rounds at specific bars) and is often inherited: sons and daughters get to know each other and form their own koadrila, following their parents' one.
