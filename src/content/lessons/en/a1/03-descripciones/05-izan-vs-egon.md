This is probably the trickiest distinction in A1 Basque. Like in Spanish "ser" vs "estar", but with two important differences: **(1) adjectives change their form** and **(2) some words are only used with one of the two verbs**.

## The rule in one line

| When you talk about… | Verb | Adjective takes `-a` |
|---|---|---|
| **What someone is like always** (quality, description) | **izan** | yes (*alaia*, *altua*) |
| **How someone is now** (state, moment) | **egon** | no (*pozik*, *nekatuta*) |

## Paired examples

See how the chosen word changes depending on whether you're talking about a quality or a state:

| Quality (izan) | State (egon) |
|---|---|
| *Alaia da.* (She is cheerful, that's her nature) | *Pozik dago.* (She is happy/content) |
| *Urduria naiz.* (I am a nervous person) | *Urduri nago.* (I am nervous today) |
| *Lasaia da.* (He is a calm person) | *Lasai dago.* (He is calm now) |
| *Ona da.* (He/She is a good person) | *Ondo dago.* (He/She is well/okay) |
| *Gaixoa da.* (He/She is sickly) | *Gaixorik dago.* (He/She is sick) |

> Note: **alaia** and **pozik** both translate to "cheerful/happy", but they are not interchangeable. *Alaia* describes character; *pozik* describes a temporary mood.

## Tips to get it right

1. **Is it happening today or are they always like this?** If it's for a specific moment → *egon*. If it's their way of being → *izan*.
2. **Does the word end in `-ik`, `-ta`, or is it an adverb (`ondo`, `gaizki`)?** It almost always goes with *egon*.
3. **Am I describing a permanent physical trait (tall, fat, young)?** It goes with *izan* + `-a`.

## Typical A1 cases

- *Ni langilea naiz, baina gaur nekatuta nago.* — I am a hard worker, but today I am tired.
- *Egoitz oso jatorra da, baina haserre dago.* — Egoitz is very nice, but he is angry.
- *Maialenen ama gaztea da. Pozik dago, gaur urtebetetzea da eta.* — Maialen's mother is young. She is happy because today is her birthday.

## Ask correctly

- **Nolakoa zara?** → describe your character / physique (use *izan*).
- **Nola zaude?** or **Zer moduz?** → say how you are today (use *egon*).

> **Note:** In Spanish, you would say "soy nervioso" or "estoy nervioso" without changing the word. In Basque, the word itself changes (*urduria* vs *urduri*). It's tricky at first, but it becomes natural with daily practice. If you're unsure in a real conversation, a safe bet is to use *egon* with an invariable word: *haserre nago*, *triste nago*, *nekatuta nago* — they always work.
