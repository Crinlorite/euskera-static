To describe hair and eyes in Basque, you don't use **izan** ("to be") like in Spanish ("I am dark-haired"), but rather **ukan** ("to have"): *I have black hair*, *I have blue eyes*.

## Hair: ile

The word for "hair" is **ile** (with the article: *ilea*). Adjectives come after it, and only the last one takes the `-a` ending:

| Euskera | English |
|---|---|
| *ile beltza* | black hair |
| *ile horia* | blond hair |
| *ile zuria* | white / gray hair |
| *ile gorria* | red hair |
| *ile urdina* | gray hair |
| *ile motza* | short hair |
| *ile luzea* | long hair |
| *ile lisoa* | straight hair |
| *ile kizkurra* | curly hair |

> In Basque, hair is treated as **singular**, just like in Spanish. That's why you use *dut* / *du* (not *ditut* / *ditu*).

- *Ile beltza eta luzea **dut**.* — I have black and long hair.
- *Maialenek ile kizkurra **du**.* — Maialen has curly hair.

## Eyes: begiak

Unlike hair, "eyes" is **plural** (*begiak*). Therefore, the adjectives are also plural (`-ak`) and you use **ditut / ditu**:

| Euskera | English |
|---|---|
| *begi urdinak* | blue eyes |
| *begi marroiak* | brown eyes |
| *begi berdeak* | green eyes |
| *begi beltzak* | black eyes |
| *begi argiak* | light eyes |
| *begi ilunak* | dark eyes |

- *Begi urdinak **ditut**.* — I have blue eyes.
- *Mikelek begi marroiak **ditu**.* — Mikel has brown eyes.

## Other features: the -dun suffix

To say "who has X" or "with X", Basque has a very productive suffix: **-duna** (singular) / **-dunak** (plural). It's added to a noun, and the person becomes "the one with glasses", "the one with the beard"...

| Euskera | English |
|---|---|
| *betaurrekoduna* | with glasses / the one with glasses |
| *bizarduna* | with a beard |
| *biboteduna* | with a mustache |
| *burusoila* | bald (literally "peeled head") |

These ones do go with **izan**, because they describe a quality, like an adjective:

- *Hura biboteduna da.* — He has a mustache.
- *Nire aitona burusoila da.* — My grandfather is bald.

## Putting it all together

A typical description mixes several structures:

> *Maialen txikia eta argala da. Ile gorria eta kizkurra du. Begi berdeak ditu. Betaurrekoduna da.*
>
> Maialen is short and thin. She has red and curly hair. She has green eyes. She wears glasses.

Notice how it's divided: **izan** for general qualities (*txikia da*), **ukan** for hair/eyes (*ile … du*, *begi … ditu*), and *-dun*-a again with **izan** for accessories.
