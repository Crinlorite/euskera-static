In Basque, the time is constructed with the verb **izan** (to be) and, except for 'one o'clock', the hour is always **in the plural**. Literally: *"they are the fives"*, *"they are the eights"*. It sounds strange at first, but the pattern is very regular.

## The question

To ask the time, you use **Zer ordu da?** ("what time is it?").

> *— Zer ordu da?*
>
> *— Zazpiak dira.* (It's seven o'clock.)

## On the hour

| Time | Basque |
|---|---|
| 1:00 | **Ordu bata da** |
| 2:00 | **Ordu biak dira** |
| 3:00 | **Hirurak dira** |
| 4:00 | **Laurak dira** |
| 5:00 | **Bostak dira** |
| 6:00 | **Seiak dira** |
| 7:00 | **Zazpiak dira** |
| 8:00 | **Zortziak dira** |
| 9:00 | **Bederatziak dira** |
| 10:00 | **Hamarrak dira** |
| 11:00 | **Hamaikak dira** |
| 12:00 | **Hamabiak dira** |

## Why one and two o'clock are special

- **Ordu bata** (one o'clock) is **singular**, so you say it with **da**: *Ordu bata da*.
- **Ordu biak** (two o'clock) has the word *ordu* before it because *biak* alone could be confusing — it's the only hour where many people add "ordu" for clarity. The others (from three o'clock onwards) stand alone: *hirurak*, *laurak*, *bostak*…

## The `-ak` suffix

Where does that final **-ak** come from? It's the Basque plural (you saw it in *gurasoak*, *aiton-amonak*). Here too: *bost* (five) → *bostak* ("the fives") + *dira* ("are").

> **Note:** many people tell time using the 12-hour clock, just like in Spanish. To distinguish between morning and afternoon, you just add *goizeko* (of the morning) or *arratsaldeko* (of the afternoon) before the time: *goizeko zortziak* (eight in the morning). We'll see this in lesson 3.

## Practice

Look at the clock on your phone right now and say the time out loud. If it's eleven: *hamaikak dira*. If it's one: *ordu bata da*. Do this all week every time you look at the clock — it's the best way to make it automatic.
