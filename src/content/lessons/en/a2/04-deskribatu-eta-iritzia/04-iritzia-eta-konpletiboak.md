For A2, expressing opinions is one of the **key skills**. This lesson gives you:

1. The formulas for starting an opinion.
2. The completive clause with **-ela** (that…).
3. Negating an opinion with **-enik** (I don't think that…).
4. The cause with **-lako** and **zeren** (because…).

## Starting an opinion

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| I think that… | *Uste dut...* |
| It seems to me that… | *Iruditzen zait...* |
| In my opinion | *Nire ustez* |
| For me | *Niretzat* |
| I know that… | *Badakit...* |
| I agree | *Ados nago* |
| I don't agree | *Ez nago ados* |
| You're right | *Arrazoia duzu* |
| It's true | *Egia da* |

## The completive clause with -**ela**

When the opinion introduces another clause, the subordinate verb takes the suffix **-(e)la** ("that…"). The change is very regular:

| Without suffix | With -ela | English |
|---|---|---|
| *da* | *dela* | that it is |
| *naiz* | *naizela* | that I am |
| *zara* | *zarela* | that you are |
| *du* | *duela* | that he/she/it has it |
| *ditu* | *dituela* | that he/she/it has them |
| *dut* | *dudala* | that I have it |
| *zen* | *zela* | that it was |
| *nuen* | *nuela* | that I had it |
| *etorriko da* | *etorriko **dela*** | that he/she/it will come |

> *Uste dut Anboto pozik **dagoela**.* — I think that Anboto is happy.
>
> *Badakit gauza zailak gertatu **direla**.* — I know that difficult things have happened.
>
> *Argi dago euskaraz hobeto hitz egiten **duela**.* — It's clear that he/she speaks better in Basque.

## Negating an opinion: -**enik**

When you say "**I don't think that** + verb", the suffix of the subordinate verb changes to **-enik**:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| I don't think it's good | *Ez dut uste ona **denik*** |
| I don't think they will come | *Ez dut uste etorriko **direnik*** |
| It doesn't seem to me that it's true | *Ez zait iruditzen egia **denik*** |

> This is the specific form of negation with verbs of opinion. In the affirmative: *uste dut ona **dela***. In the negative: *ez dut uste ona **denik***.

## Other opinion structures

These structures combine adjective + *da* + clause with -ela:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| It's clear that… | *Argi dago + -(e)la* |
| It's important to… | *Garrantzitsua da + -tzea* |
| It's true that… | *Egia da + -(e)la* |
| It's possible to… | *Posible da + -tzea* |
| It's logical that… | *Logikoa da + -(e)la* |
| It's interesting to… | *Interesgarria da + -tzea* |

> *Argi dago ikasten ari **zarela**.* — It's clear that you are studying.
>
> *Garrantzitsua da euskaraz **hitz egitea**.* — It's important to speak in Basque.

## Causal clauses: *-lako* and *zeren*

To express **cause** ("because"), you have two ways:

### Suffix -**lako** (subordinate, the most common)

> **verb + -lako**

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Because he/she/it has come | *Etorri **delako*** |
| Because I'm hungry | *Goseak nagoelako* (o *gose naizelako*) |
| Because it's important | *Garrantzitsua **delako*** |
| Because I don't like it | *Ez zaidalako gustatzen* |

> *Etxean nago **gaixorik nagoelako**.* — I'm at home because I'm sick.
>
> *Bilbora joaten naiz lana **bertan dudalako**.* — I go to Bilbao because I have my job there.

### Conjunction *zeren* (at the beginning of the clause)

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Because it is important | *…, **zeren** garrantzitsua baita* |
| …, since he/she/it has come | *…, **zeren** etorri baita* |

> This is the "Latin-style" way of expressing cause, more formal or journalistic. In speech, *-lako* is preferred.

## Mini-debate

> *— Olatz, **uste dut** Bilbo Donostia baino interesgarriagoa **dela**.*
>
> *— **Ez nago ados**. Donostia askoz politagoa da, **uste dut**, itsasoa **duelako** ondoan.*
>
> *— **Egia da** itsasoa duela, baina Bilbon **museo gehiago daudela ere egia da**.*
>
> *— Bai, baina **iruditzen zait** giroa, kalea, jendea... Donostian goxoagoak **direla**. Eta Donostiako pintxoak Bilbokoak **baino hobeak direla** ere bai!*

## Useful structures in a list

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| I think it's important | *Garrantzitsua **dela** uste dut* |
| I know he/she is right | *Arrazoia **duela** badakit* |
| I don't think they will come | *Ez dut uste etorriko **direnik*** |
| It's clear that he/she has problems | *Argi dago arazoak **dituela*** |
| Because he/she is sick | *Gaixorik **dagoelako*** |
| Because it's cold | *Hotz **egiten duelako*** |

## Common pitfalls

1. **Don't forget the two sides: -ela in the affirmative, -enik in a negative opinion.**
2. **The suffix -ela attaches to the conjugated verb**, not the participle: *etorriko da → etorriko **dela***.
3. **In Spanish, the strength of an opinion (or doubt) can trigger the subjunctive**, but in Basque there's no such sharp distinction: *uste dut ondo dagoela* (I think it's okay) and *zalantza dut ondo dagoenik* (I doubt that it's okay) use different forms, but there isn't a formal "subjunctive" mode.

> **Teaching note:** the formula *Nire ustez + clause + -lako + clause* is probably the most useful A2-level tool for giving and arguing an opinion. Model example:
>
> *Nire ustez **euskara ikastea garrantzitsua da** **gure herriaren hizkuntza delako**.*
> ("In my opinion, learning Basque is important because it is the language of our people".)
