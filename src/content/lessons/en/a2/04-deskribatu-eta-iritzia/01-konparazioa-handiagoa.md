Comparing is one of the most common acts of language: *this one is bigger than that one*, *I am shorter than you*, *the omelette is better than yesterday*. In Basque, the formula is very regular and, once you master it, it opens up a lot of new sentences.

## The formula

> **X baino Y-AGO(A)**

- **baino** is placed between the two compared terms — it's equivalent to "than".
- **-ago** is added to the adjective or adverb (like the English "-er" or the Spanish comparative marker).
- If it goes with a definite noun, the adjective takes its usual **-a**: *altuago**a***.

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Bigger | *handi**agoa*** |
| Smaller | *txiki**agoa*** |
| More expensive | *garesti**agoa*** |
| Prettier | *polit**agoa*** |
| Faster | *azkar**agoa*** |

## Example sentences

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Lander is taller than Olatz | *Lander Olatz **baino** altu**agoa** da* |
| This house is bigger than that one | *Etxe hau hura **baino** handi**agoa** da* |
| Bilbao is bigger than Donosti | *Bilbo Donostia **baino** handi**agoa** da* |
| The coffee is more expensive than the tea | *Kafea tea **baino** garesti**ago** dago* |
| Garazi runs faster than me | *Garazik nik **baino** azkarrago **egiten du korrika*** |

> **Word order:** in Basque, the "than" (*baino*) goes between the two things being compared, not after the adjective. **X baino Y-agoa** is literally "X than Y-more".

## Irregular adjectives

Some adjectives don't follow the regular rule and are worth memorizing:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Good → better | *ona → **hobea*** |
| Bad → worse | *txarra → **okerragoa*** |
| Much/Many → more | *asko → **gehiago*** |
| Little/Few → less | *gutxi → **gutxiago*** |

> *Liburu hau hura **baino hobea** da.* — This book is better than that one.
>
> *Garazik nik **baino gehiago** ikasten du.* — Garazi studies more than I do.

## Comparative with verbs

When you compare **actions** or **quantities**, *gehiago / gutxiago* works as an adverb:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| I work more than you | *Zuk **baino gehiago** lan egiten dut* |
| He/She studies less than before | *Lehen **baino gutxiago** ikasten du* |
| I eat more fruit than vegetables | *Barazkiak **baino fruta gehiago** jaten dut* |

> *Gehiago* and *gutxiago* go **before** the verb or the modified noun, and *baino* keeps its place between the two terms.

## Comparative with adverbs

| Adverb | Comparative |
|---|---|
| *azkar* (fast) | *azkarrago* |
| *poliki* (slowly) | *polikiago* |
| *ondo* (well) | *hobeto* (irregular) |
| *gaizki* (badly) | *okerrago* |
| *berandu* (late) | *beranduago* |
| *goiz* (early) | *goizago* |

> *Hobeto egiten duzu lan.* — You work better.
>
> *Goizago etorriko naiz bihar.* — I will come earlier tomorrow.

## Negation: "less than"

For "less than", the natural formula is **gutxiago + baino**:

> *Lander nik baino **gutxiago** lan egiten du.* — Lander works less than I do.

Or with the comparative inverted:

> *Olatz Lander baino **txikiagoa** da.* (= Lander is taller than Olatz, formulated the other way around.)

## Negations with *hain* — "not as"

A very productive formula:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| It's not as expensive | *Ez da **hain** garestia* |
| Not as fast | *Ez **hain** azkar* |
| It's not as big as you thought | *Ez da uste zenuen **bezain** handia* |

> *Hain* is used for "so" or "as" (= this much). Combined with *ez* it works to soften comparisons.

## Mini-dialogue

> *— Anboto, etxe berria zer moduz?*
>
> *— Aurrekoa **baino handiagoa** da, eta argia ere **askoz argiagoa** da. Baina logelak **txikiagoak** dira.*
>
> *— Eta kalea?*
>
> *— Lehen baino **lasaiagoa**: ez dago **hainbeste** autorik.*

## Mnemonic trick

Memorize the formula as a block:

> **X baino Y-agoa da.**

And for the irregulars: **hobea, okerragoa, gehiago, gutxiago**. With that, you cover 95% of A2-level superiority comparisons.

> **Cultural note:** in everyday speech, you'll also hear reinforced expressions: *askoz handiagoa* (much bigger), *pixka bat handiagoa* (a little bigger), *dezente handiagoa* (quite a bit bigger). These are nuances that, while not strictly A2, pepper every conversation.
