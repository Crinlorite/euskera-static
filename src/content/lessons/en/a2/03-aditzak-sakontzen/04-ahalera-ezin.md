To say "I can / he/she can / we can" in Basque, you have two options: a **formal** one (*ahalera* with a special auxiliary) and a **colloquial** one (*ahal* with a normal auxiliary). At A2 level, it's useful to recognize both.

For "cannot", there is also a dedicated word: **ezin**.

## Option 1: the colloquial form — *ahal*

The simplest one and the one you'll hear most often:

> **partizipioa + ahal + auxiliar**

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Can you come? | *Etorri **ahal zara**?* |
| Can I come in? | *Sartu **ahal naiz**?* |
| Can you help me? | *Lagundu **ahal didazu**?* |
| Can we talk to you? | *Hitz egin **ahal dugu** zurekin?* |

> In written or more formal registers, **ahal izan** appears: *etorri ahal izango da*, *jakin ahal izan dut*. In spoken language, *ahal* + auxiliary works.

## Option 2: the "true" *ahalera* — *dezaket / naiteke*

This is the synthetic form of the potential mood. It has two series, depending on whether the verb is transitive or intransitive.

### Transitive verbs (NOR-NORK): *dezaket*

| Pronoun | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| nik | **dezaket** | I can (do) it |
| zuk | **dezakezu** | you can (do) it |
| hark | **dezake** | he/she can (do) it |
| guk | **dezakegu** | we can (do) it |
| zuek | **dezakezue** | you (pl.) can (do) it |
| haiek | **dezakete** | they can (do) it |

> **participle without ending + dezaket**: *egin → egin dezaket*, *idatzi → idatz dezaket*, *jan → jan dezaket*.

### Intransitive verbs (NOR): *naiteke*

| Pronoun | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **naiteke** | I can |
| zu | **zaitezke** | you can |
| hura | **daiteke** | he/she can |
| gu | **gaitezke** | we can |
| zuek | **zaitezkete** | you (pl.) can |
| haiek | **daitezke** | they can |

> *Etor naiteke?* — Can I come?
>
> *Joan zaitezke?* — Can you go?
>
> *Hori egia izan daiteke.* — That can be true.

## The synthetic formula

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Transitive | participle (without typical final ending) + *dezaket* etc. | *Liburua **irakur dezakezu*** (you can read the book) |
| Intransitive | participle + *naiteke* etc. | *Etor **zaitezke**?* (can you come?) |

> **Spelling detail:** in the *ahalera*, the participle appears "clipped" for some verbs: *jan → jan*, *egin → egin*, *irakurri → irakur*, *ikusi → ikus*, *etorri → etor*. The final -i ending is dropped. It's a typical formal marker.

## *Ezin* — cannot

The negation of the *ahalera* has its own word: **ezin**.

> **ezin + partizipioa + auxiliar**

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| I can't come | *Ezin **dut etorri*** (NOR-NORK) or *Ezin **naiz etorri*** (NOR) |
| I can't go | *Ezin naiz joan* |
| You can't do that | *Ezin duzu hori egin* |
| I couldn't sleep | *Ezin nuen lo egin* |
| One can't know | *Ezin da jakin* |
| We can't wait | *Ezin dugu itxaron* |

> *Ezin* follows the subject and is followed by the participle + auxiliary. It's very productive.

## In the past tense: *nezakeen / nintekeen / ezin nuen*

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **nezakeen** | I could (do) it (past potential) |
| **zezakeen** | he/she could (do) it |
| **nintekeen** | I could (past NOR) |
| **ezin nintzen** | I couldn't (NOR) |
| **ezin nuen** | I couldn't (NOR-NORK) |

> *Atzo ezin nintzen joan, gaixorik nengoen.* — Yesterday I couldn't go, I was sick.

## Modal vs. auxiliary: three ways to say "you can do it"

1. **Colloquial:** *Egin ahal duzu.* — The most natural in speech.
2. **Synthetic:** *Egin dezakezu.* — More formal or written.
3. **Negative with ezin:** *Ezin duzu egin.* — For "you can't do it".

All three coexist. At A2, you should be able to produce the first two and recognize all of them.

## *Ahal izan* in other tenses

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| I have been able to go | *Joan ahal izan naiz* |
| I will be able to go | *Joan ahal izango naiz* |
| I haven't been able to | *Ezin izan dut* |

## Everyday examples

- *Lagundu ahal didazu, mesedez?* — Can you help me?
- *Bihar ezin naiz etorri.* — I can't come tomorrow.
- *Hau uler dezaket.* — I can understand this.
- *Ezin duzu hori egin.* — You can't do that.
- *Goazen lasai, denbora dugu eta.* — Let's take it easy, we have time.
- *Etxea bihar eros daiteke.* — The house can be bought tomorrow.

## Mini-dialogue

> *— Anboto, **etor zaitezke** bihar afaltzera?*
>
> *— Tira, **ezin dut**: lan asko daukat eta berandu arte ezin naiz mugitu. Baina hurrengo astean **etor naiteke**, eta zer ekar **dezaket**?*
>
> *— **Ekar dezakezu** ardo botila bat, hori asko gustatzen zaigu.*

> **Teaching note:** at A2 level, the most useful thing is to handle *ezin dut/naiz* for "I can't" and *ahal duzu?* for asking questions well. You will integrate the synthetic forms (*dezaket, naiteke*) through reading. The B1 unit goes into them in more depth.
