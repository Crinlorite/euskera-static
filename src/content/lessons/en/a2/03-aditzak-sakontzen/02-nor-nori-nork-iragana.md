The NOR-NORI-NORK past tense follows the same logic as the present, but with **past** tense markers (the endings in -**n** and the changing initial letter).

## The formula

> **partizipioa + n-i-NORI-NORK + n** (at the end)

Compared to the present tense, what changes is:

- The initial **d-** becomes **n-** or **z-** (just like *dut → nuen*).
- A final **-n** appears (the classic past tense marker).

## The most useful forms for A2

Past tense table, singular NOR ("it"):

| NORI ↓ / NORK → | nik | zuk | hark | guk | zuek | haiek |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| niri | — | **zenidan** | **zidan** | — | **zenidaten** | **zidaten** |
| zuri | **nizun** | — | **zizun** | **genizun** | — | **zizuten** |
| hari | **nion** | **zenion** | **zion** | **genion** | **zenioten** | **zioten** |
| guri | — | **zenigun** | **zigun** | — | **zeniguten** | **ziguten** |
| zuei | **nizuen** | — | **zizuen** | **genizuen** | — | **zizueten** |
| haiei | **nien** | **zenien** | **zien** | **genien** | **zenieten** | **zieten** |

> The four cells you'll use most at A2 level: **nion (I... to him/her), zion (he/she... to him/her), nizun (I... to you), zidan (he/she... to me)**. The others are built from these.

## Mnemonic tip

Compare present and past:

| Present | Past | English |
|---|---|---|
| *dizut* | **nizun** | I... it to you |
| *diot* | **nion** | I... it to him/her |
| *dit* | **zidan** | he/she... it to me |
| *dio* | **zion** | he/she... it to him/her |
| *digu* | **zigun** | he/she... it to us |
| *digute* | **ziguten** | they... it to us |

> **Pattern:** the initial letter changes (d → n/z), the NORI infix stays similar, and you add **-n** at the end.

## Agreement with plural NOR

| Singular object | Plural object | English |
|---|---|---|
| nion | **nizkion** | I... it to him/her / I... them to him/her |
| zion | **zizkion** | he/she... it to him/her / he/she... them to him/her |
| zidan | **zizkidan** | he/she... it to me / he/she... them to me |
| genion | **genizkion** | we... it to him/her / we... them to him/her |

> *Aitak liburu bi **eman zizkidan** atzo.* — My father gave me two books yesterday.

## Special verbs with NOR-NORI-NORK past

Some everyday verbs almost always use NOR-NORI-NORK:

- *Esan zion* — he/she told him/her
- *Eman zidan* — he/she gave me
- *Galdetu zion* — he/she asked him/her
- *Erantzun nion* — I answered him/her
- *Ekarri ziguten* — they brought us
- *Erosi nion* — I bought for him/her

## Mini-narrative

> *Atzo Garazi etorri zen etxera. Ate ondoan zegoela, **eman zidan** opari bat: kafe-poltsatxo eder bat. **Esan zidan** Bilbon erosi zuela, kafetegi berezi batean. Eskerrak **eman nizkion** eta laster **prestatu nion** kafe bat berari ere. Lasai-lasai **kontatu zidan** azken aldian Donostian zegoela bizitzen.*

## Mixing with NOR and NOR-NORK

An A2 narrative mixes the three patterns:

| Phrase | Type |
|---|---|
| *Etorri zen* | NOR past |
| *Eman zidan* | NOR-NORI-NORK past |
| *Esan zidan* | NOR-NORI-NORK past |
| *Erosi zuen* | NOR-NORK past |
| *Kontatu zidan* | NOR-NORI-NORK past |

> **Tip:** if the sentence answers "what did he/she do?" → NOR-NORK (*dut/nuen*). If it answers "what did he/she do to whom?" → NOR-NORI-NORK (*diot/nion*). If it answers "what happened to him/her?" without a direct object → NOR (*naiz/nintzen*).

## Common pitfalls

1. **Distinguish *zion* (he/she gave him/her, past) from *zen* (was, past).** They sound similar; the context and the presence of an object + recipient will help you.
2. **Negation uses *ez* before the verb**: *Ez nion ezer esan* (I didn't tell him/her anything). With a negated object, *-rik* appears: *Ez nion liburu**rik** eman*.
3. **To speak at an A2 level**, you just need **nion, zion, zidan, nizun**. The other forms can be recognized when reading.

> **Pedagogical note:** the NOR-NORI-NORK past tense table is dense, but its payoff is huge: it opens up narration with three arguments, which is very common in any story. If you memorize just one model verb (for example *eman*), the other forms are copied from it.
