You already know the **recent past** (*joan naiz*, *jan dut*) from A1. In A2 you take a decisive step: the **complete past** (*iragan burutua*). It's the equivalent of "I was / I did / I said" that you use to narrate closed, distant, or long-past events.

This first lesson focuses on NOR verbs: **izan** and **egon** in the past tense.

## Past tense of *izan* (to be)

| Pronoun | Past | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **nintzen** | I was |
| zu | **zinen** | you were |
| hura | **zen** | he/she/it was |
| gu | **ginen** | we were |
| zuek | **zineten** | you (pl.) were |
| haiek | **ziren** | they were |

> Tip: the final **n** appears in all forms (n**intzen**, zi**nen**, ze**n**, gi**nen**, zi**ne**ten, zir**en**). It's the classic mark of the past tense in Basque.

## Past tense of *egon* (to be) — synthetic

*Egon* has synthetic forms in the past tense, parallel to the present tense *nago/zaude/dago...*:

| Pronoun | Past | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **nengoen** | I was |
| zu | **zeunden** | you were |
| hura | **zegoen** | he/she/it was |
| gu | **geunden** | we were |
| zuek | **zeundeten** | you (pl.) were |
| haiek | **zeuden** | they were |

> In everyday speech, it's also valid to use **egon nintzen / egon zen** etc., with an auxiliary. The synthetic forms sound more polished or literary, but you will hear them a lot.

## Distinction: past *izan* vs. past *egon*

Just like in the present tense, in the past tense you maintain the distinction between:

- **Quality / identity** → *izan*: *Egoitz medikua zen* (Egoitz was a doctor).
- **Temporary state / location** → *egon*: *Egoitz pozik zegoen* (Egoitz was happy) / *Egoitz Bilbon zegoen* (Egoitz was in Bilbao).

## Complete past markers

To make the sentence sound like a closed past event, Basque uses time markers:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Yesterday | *Atzo* |
| Last night | *Bart* |
| The day before yesterday | *Herenegun* |
| Last week | *Joan den astean* / *Aurreko astean* |
| Last month | *Joan den hilean* |
| Last year | *Joan den urtean* / *Iaz* |
| Two years ago | *Duela bi urte* |
| A long time ago | *Aspaldian* / *Duela urte asko* |
| Then | *Orduan* |
| In 2010 | *2010ean* |

## Typical patterns

> *Atzo ni etxean **nintzen** goiz osoan.* — Yesterday I was at home all morning.
>
> *Joan den urtean Garazi Frantzian **zegoen**.* — Last year Garazi was in France.
>
> *Duela hamar urte ikaskideak **ginen**.* — Ten years ago we were classmates.
>
> *Bart kalean jende asko **zegoen**.* — Last night there were a lot of people in the street.
>
> *Haurrak gu **ginen**, eta orain helduak gara.* — We were the children, and now we are adults.

## Negation

Just like in the present tense: **ez** before the verb.

> *Atzo ez nintzen lanean egon.* — Yesterday I wasn't at work.
>
> *Ainara ez zegoen pozik orduan.* — Ainara wasn't happy then.
>
> *Gu ez ginen ikaskideak.* — We weren't classmates.

## Mini-text

> *Duela hamar urte Anboto Bartzelonan **bizi zen**. Han ikaslea **zen** eta unibertsitateko logelan **zegoen**. Pozik **zegoen** baina urruti **zegoen** familiatik. Astebururo telefonoz hitz egiten zuen amarekin.*

> **Note:** in this passage, *zen* (was) and *zegoen* (was) coexist, along with other forms we will see in the next lessons. That is the heart of unit 2: weaving together past forms to narrate.
