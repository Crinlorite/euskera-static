The Basque **imperfect** (*iragan ez burutua*) roughly corresponds to the English "I was eating / I lived / I used to go / I was working". It's used to talk about:

- **Past habits** (what you used to do).
- **Descriptions** (what something was like, where you lived, what you had).
- **Ongoing actions** that serve as a backdrop for another specific action.

## The formula

> **verb in -tzen / -ten + auxiliary in the past**

Compare present vs. imperfect:

| Present (habit today) | Imperfect (habit yesterday) |
|---|---|
| *Lan egiten dut* (I work) | *Lan egiten **nuen*** (I used to work) |
| *Bilbon bizi naiz* (I live) | *Bilbon bizi **nintzen*** (I lived) |
| *Filmak ikusten ditut* (I watch) | *Filmak ikusten **nituen*** (I used to watch) |
| *Egunero joaten naiz* (I go) | *Egunero **joaten nintzen*** (I used to go) |

> The main verb is in its **habitual** form (-tzen / -ten — the same one from A1) and the auxiliary is in the **past** (the forms from lessons 1 and 2).

## Conjugation with the NOR auxiliary (intransitives)

| Pronoun | Imperfect | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | *…tzen* **nintzen** | I was going/was/lived |
| zu | *…tzen* **zinen** | you were going |
| hura | *…tzen* **zen** | he/she was going/was |
| gu | *…tzen* **ginen** | we were going/were |
| zuek | *…tzen* **zineten** | you (pl.) were going |
| haiek | *…tzen* **ziren** | they were going |

> *Garai hartan Donostian **bizi nintzen**.* — At that time, I lived in San Sebastián.

## Conjugation with the NOR-NORK auxiliary (transitives)

| Pronoun | Imperfect | English |
|---|---|---|
| nik | *…tzen* **nuen** | I was doing it |
| zuk | *…tzen* **zenuen** | you were doing it |
| hark | *…tzen* **zuen** | he/she was doing it |
| guk | *…tzen* **genuen** | we were doing it |
| zuek | *…tzen* **zenuten** | you (pl.) were doing it |
| haiek | *…tzen* **zuten** | they were doing it |

> *Aitak afari berezia **prestatzen zuen** igandero.* — The father used to prepare a special dinner every Sunday.

## Burutua vs ez burutua: the key opposition

| Type | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Burutua (completed) | participle + past auxiliary | specific, completed event |
| Ez burutua (imperfect) | -tzen + past auxiliary | habit or ongoing action |

Compare:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| Yesterday I went to work | *Atzo lanera **joan nintzen*** |
| Before, I used to go to work by bike | *Lehen lanera bizikletaz **joaten nintzen*** |
| I saw a movie on Saturday | *Larunbatean film bat **ikusi nuen*** |
| I used to watch movies every Saturday | *Larunbatero filmak **ikusten nituen*** |

## Progressive aspect: *ari nintzen*

To say "I was doing," you use the periphrasis with **ari**:

> **-tzen + ari + izan in the past** (NOR)

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| I was eating | *Jaten **ari nintzen*** |
| We were working | *Lanean **ari ginen*** |
| It was raining | *Euria **ari zuen*** |
| What were you doing? | *Zer **ari zinen** egiten?* |

> Just like *ari naiz* in the present, it's *ari nintzen* in the past. It's used to indicate that the action was **in progress** at a specific moment.

## Markers that call for the imperfect

These markers push the sentence towards the imperfect/habitual:

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| As a child | *Txikitan* |
| When I was young | *Gaztea nintzenean* |
| At that time | *Garai hartan* |
| Before / in the old days | *Lehen* / *Lehenago* |
| Always | *Beti* |
| Every day | *Egunero* |
| Often | *Sarri* / *Askotan* |

## Comparative mini-text

> **Burutua:** *Atzo Iratiren etxera **joan nintzen**, kafea **hartu nuen**, eta hamabietan **itzuli nintzen**.* — Yesterday I went to Irati's house, had a coffee, and came back at twelve.
>
> **Ez burutua:** *Lehen Iratiren etxera **joaten nintzen** astero. Han kafea **hartzen genuen** eta luze **hitz egiten genuen**.* — Before, I used to go to Irati's house every week. There we would have coffee and talk for a long time.
>
> **Progresivoa:** *Atzo, kafea **hartzen ari ginela**, telefonoa jo zuen.* — Yesterday, while we were having coffee, the phone rang.

## Common pitfalls

1. **Regular verb + -tzen** works almost always. Be careful: *jan → jaten* (not *jantzen*), *edan → edaten*, *egin → egiten*. These are memorized in A1.
2. **Don't literally translate "was being"**: in Basque, the imperfect covers many uses of the descriptive past, without needing to add *ari*.
3. **The auxiliary is the same** as with the participle (*nintzen, nuen*); the only thing that changes is the form of the main verb.

> **Practical summary:** if the sentence answers "what happened?" → *participle + auxiliary* (burutua). If it answers "what did you used to do?" or "what was it like?" → *-tzen + past auxiliary* (ez burutua). If it answers "what were you doing at that moment?" → *-tzen ari + izan past* (puntukaria).
