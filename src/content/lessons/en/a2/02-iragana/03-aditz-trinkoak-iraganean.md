**Synthetic verbs** (*aditz trinkoak*) are the few Basque verbs that are conjugated **without an auxiliary**, in a single word. You already know their present tense forms: *noa, nator, nabil, daukat, dakit, nago*. In the past tense, they have parallel forms that are good to recognize and, for the most common ones, to master.

## Visual summary

For the six key synthetic verbs, here's how the past tense looks for the 1st and 3rd person:

| Verb | Present (I) | Past (I) | Past (he/she) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *joan* (to go) | noa | **nindoan** | **zihoan** |
| *etorri* (to come) | nator | **nentorren** | **zetorren** |
| *ibili* (to walk) | nabil | **nenbilen** | **zebilen** |
| *egon* (to be) | nago | **nengoen** | **zegoen** |
| *eduki* (to have) | daukat | **neukan** | **zeukan** |
| *jakin* (to know) | dakit | **nekien** | **zekien** |

> What they have in common: they start with **n-** (I) or **z-** (3rd person, formal you, or you all) and end in **-en**. This is the general pattern for the synthetic past.

## *Joan* (to go) — full past tense

| Pronoun | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **nindoan** | I was going / I went (ongoing) |
| zu | **zindoazen** | you were going / you went |
| hura | **zihoan** | he/she was going / went |
| gu | **gindoazen** | we were going |
| zuek | **zindoazten** | you (pl.) were going |
| haiek | **zihoazen** | they were going |

> *Egunero zortzietan lanera **nindoan**.* — I used to go to work at eight every day.

## *Etorri* (to come)

| Pronoun | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **nentorren** | I was coming |
| zu | **zentozen** | you were coming |
| hura | **zetorren** | he/she was coming |
| gu | **gentozen** | we were coming |
| zuek | **zentozten** | you (pl.) were coming |
| haiek | **zetozen** | they were coming |

> *Lagunak Donostiatik **zetozen**.* — The friends were coming from San Sebastián.

## *Ibili* (to walk)

| Pronoun | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| ni | **nenbilen** | I was walking |
| hura | **zebilen** | he/she was walking |
| gu | **genbiltzan** | we were walking |
| haiek | **zebiltzan** | they were walking |

> *Garazi mendian **zebilen** lagunekin.* — Garazi was walking in the mountains with her friends.

## When do I use *nindoan* vs. *joan nintzen*?

That's the key question. Roughly:

| Form | Type of past | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Joan nintzen** (analytic + participle) | Specific, finished, complete event | *Atzo Bilbora **joan nintzen** zortzietan.* (= yesterday I went to Bilbao) |
| **Nindoan** (synthetic) | Ongoing action, habit, description of the past | *Bilborantz **nindoan**, ezagun bat ikusi nuenean.* (= I was going towards Bilbao when I saw an acquaintance) |

> **Quick rule:** the synthetic form (*nindoan, nentorren, nenbilen*) describes the **scene** in the past. The form with a participle (*joan nintzen, etorri nintzen*) narrates the **event** in the past. The difference is very similar to 'was going' vs. 'went' in English.

## NOR-NORK synthetic verbs: *neukan*, *nekien*

We remember these two from the previous lesson because they also belong to the *trinkoak* family:

> *Egoitzek bizikleta gorri bat **zeukan**.* — Egoitz had a red bike.
>
> *Inork ez **zekien** zergatik etorri zen.* — Nobody knew why he/she came.

Other NOR-NORK synthetic verbs (*ekarri/zekarren*, *eraman/zeraman*) appear less frequently in everyday A2 level Basque; it's enough to recognize them.

## Mini-text

> *Umea nintzenean, oporretan beti aiton-amonen baserrira **nindoan**. Han **nengoen** astebete edo bi. Goizean, lehengusuak **etorri ohi ziren** eta egun osoa elkarrekin **igarotzen genuen**: txakurrarekin mendi inguruan **genbiltzan**, sagastian sagarrak **biltzen genituen** eta amonak gosari ederra **prestatzen zigun**. Pozik **nengoen** beti.*

> **Teaching note:** it's not necessary to master all synthetic forms at the A2 level. The important thing is to **recognize them** when they appear and be able to use at least *nengoen, zegoen, neukan, zeukan, nekien, zekien, nindoan, zihoan, nenbilen, zebilen*. The rest will stick with reading.
