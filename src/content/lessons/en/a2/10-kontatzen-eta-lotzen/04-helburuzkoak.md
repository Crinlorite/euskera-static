Second to last lesson of the level: expressing **purpose** — what something is for and what you're going to do. Two sibling suffixes, with a very clear rule to tell them apart.

## -TZEKO: "for" / "to" (purpose, use)

> *Betaurrekoak **irakurtzeko** dira.* — The glasses are for reading.
>
> *Euskara **ikasteko** aplikazioa.* — An app for learning Basque. (Hello. 👋)
>
> *Denbora **aurrezteko**, trenez joango gara.* — To save time, we'll go by train.
>
> *Ez daukat dirurik **sarrera ordaintzeko**.* — I don't have money to pay for the ticket.

The question: ***Zertarako**?* (what for?) → *Horretarako* (for that).

## -TZERA: "to" (with verbs of movement)

When there's movement — *joan, etorri, atera, igo…* — Basque uses **-tzera**:

> *Ogia **erostera** noa.* — I'm going to buy bread.
>
> *Euskara **ikastera** etorri naiz.* — I've come to learn Basque.
>
> *Lagunak **bisitatzera** joango gara.* — We will go to visit our friends.
>
> *Atera zaitez **arnasa hartzera**!* — Go out to get some air!

The question: ***Zertara** etorri zara?* (what have you come to do?) → *Laguntzera!* (to help!)

## The rule for choosing (cheat sheet)

| Is there movement? | Suffix | Example |
|---|---|---|
| No — it's a "for" | **-tzeko** | *Hau ikasteko da* |
| Yes — you go/come TO | **-tzera** | *Ikastera noa* |

> Classic trap for Spanish speakers: "voy a comprar" is NOT the future tense in Basque — it's movement: *erostera noa*. You already know the real future tense: *erosiko dut*.
