The travel unit begins. First stop: **means of transport** and the verbs to use them. With the instrumental *-z* that you already know from A1, saying "by train" or "by bike" is just a suffix.

## The means

| English | Basque | "By…" (instrumental) |
|---|---|---|
| The train | *trena* | *trenez* |
| The bus | *autobusa* | *autobusez* |
| The plane | *hegazkina* | *hegazkinez* |
| The boat | *itsasontzia* | *itsasontziz* |
| The car | *kotxea / autoa* | *kotxez / autoz* |
| The bicycle | *bizikleta* | *bizikletaz* |
| On foot | — | *oinez* |

## The places

| English | Basque |
|---|---|
| The station (train/bus) | *(tren-/autobus-)geltokia* |
| The airport | *aireportua* |
| The port | *portua* |
| The taxi stand | *taxi-geltokia* |
| The road | *errepidea* |

## Travel verbs

> *Trena **hartu** dut.* — I've taken the train.
>
> *Autobusa **galdu** dugu!* — We've missed the bus!
>
> *Hegazkina berandu **atera da**.* — The plane has left late.
>
> *Trena Gasteizen **gelditzen da**.* — The train stops in Vitoria.
>
> *Bidaiariak trenera **igo dira**.* — The passengers have gotten on the train.
>
> *Donostian **jaitsiko gara**.* — We will get off in Donostia.

*Igo* (to get on/go up) and *jaitsi* (to get off/go down) are the key pair: *trenera igo* (to get on the train), *trenetik jaitsi* (to get off the train). Notice the cases: **-ra** to get ON, **-tik** to get OFF.

## How do you go?

> *— **Nola joaten zara** lanera?*
>
> *— Normalean **autobusez**, baina eguraldi ona badago, **bizikletaz** edo **oinez**. Eta zu?*
>
> *— Ni **trenez**. Etxetik geltokira bost minutu ditut **oinez**.*

> **Note:** in the Basque Country, the local commuter train has always been the *topoa* (the "mole", between Donostia and Hendaia, because it goes underground) and *Euskotren* connects the coast. If you hear *«topoa hartuko dugu»*, you know what it means: the little green train.
