Until now, you knew how to talk about the present and the past. This unit opens the third door: **the future** (*geroaldia*). The good news: if you already master the participles (and you've mastered them since A1!), the future is almost a gift — it's built **on the participle**, with no new auxiliary forms.

## The formula

> **participle + -ko / -go + auxiliary in the PRESENT**

| Spanish | Basque | How it's formed |
|---|---|---|
| I will go | *joango naiz* | joan + **-go** + naiz |
| I will eat | *jango dut* | jan + **-go** + dut |
| I will buy | *erosiko dut* | erosi + **-ko** + dut |
| We will see it | *ikusiko dugu* | ikusi + **-ko** + dugu |
| He/she/it will arrive | *helduko da* | heldu + **-ko** + da |

**The auxiliary doesn't change**: it's the same *naiz/da/dut/dugu…* as always. The only new thing is the suffix on the participle.

## -ko or -go? The only rule you need to memorize

| Participle ends in… | Suffix | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-n** | **-go** (the n is kept) | joan → joa**ngo** · egin → egi**ngo** · esan → esa**ngo** · eman → ema**ngo** · edan → eda**ngo** · jan → ja**ngo** |
| vowel or consonant (everything else) | **-ko** | erosi → erosi**ko** · hartu → hartu**ko** · ikusi → ikusi**ko** · bukatu → bukatu**ko** · heldu → heldu**ko** |

> **Dialect note:** in Navarre and Iparralde you'll hear *eginen, joanen* (-en instead of -go). This is correct in those dialects; in the standard Basque used in this course, we use **-go**.

## The big three: izan, ukan, egon

| Present | Future | Example |
|---|---|---|
| naiz (I am) | **izango naiz** | *Irakasle izango naiz.* — I will be a teacher. |
| dut (I have) | **izango dut** | *Denbora izango dut.* — I will have time. |
| nago (I am [located]) | **egongo naiz** | *Etxean egongo naiz.* — I will be at home. |

Notice: *izan* is used for both **to be** and **to have** — the auxiliary distinguishes them (*izango naiz* / *izango dut*).

## Examples in context

> *Bihar goizean kirola **egingo dut**, eta gero lagunekin **bazkalduko dut**.* — Tomorrow morning I will do sport, and then I will have lunch with friends.
>
> *Datorren astean amona **ikusiko dugu**.* — Next week we will see grandma.
>
> *Ez kezkatu, garaiz **helduko gara**.* — Don't worry, we will arrive on time.
>
> *Noiz **esango diozu** egia?* — When will you tell him/her the truth?

## Dialogue

> *— Zer **egingo duzu** gaur arratsaldean?*
>
> *— Lehenengo etxeko lanak **bukatuko ditut**, eta gero zinemara **joango naiz**. Etorri nahi?*
>
> *— Ezin dut, bihar azterketa daukat. Etxean **geratuko naiz** ikasten.*
>
> *— Lasai, hurrengoan **joango gara** elkarrekin.*

## Study tip

The Basque future is like the "going to + verb" construction in English in one sense: **you don't need to learn new conjugations**, just the *-ko/-go* piece. If you're in doubt, think of the participle (which you already know from the past tense: *erosi dut* = I have bought it) and attach the suffix: *erosiko dut*.
