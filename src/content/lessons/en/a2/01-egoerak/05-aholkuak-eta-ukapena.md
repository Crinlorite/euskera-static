This lesson integrates the three topics of the unit: we give **advice**, prohibitions, and recommendations for a healthy life. You're going to learn the **negative imperative** and a handful of very productive A2 formulas for giving opinions on what should be done.

## Negative imperative: *ez* + participle

The simplest form and the one you'll use most:

> **ez + participle**

| Spanish | Basque |
|---|---|
| Don't smoke | *Ez erre* |
| Don't drink alcohol | *Ez edan alkoholik* |
| Don't eat too much | *Ez gehiegi jan* / *Ez jan gehiegi* |
| Don't move | *Ez mugitu* |
| Don't shout | *Ez oihu egin* |
| Don't be afraid | *Ez izan beldurrik* |
| Don't run | *Ez korrika egin* |

> The partitive *-rik* appears frequently: *ez edan alkohol**ik***, *ez izan beldur**rik***. It's similar to adding "any" in English: "don't drink (any) alcohol". You saw it briefly in A1, and by A2, it's a good idea to be able to recognize it.

## Emphatic negative imperative: *ez ezazu*

In more formal texts or when you want to emphasize the prohibition, the form with *ezazu* appears:

| Spanish | Basque |
|---|---|
| Don't take this | *Ez ezazu hartu hau* |
| Don't forget | *Ez ezazu ahaztu* |
| Don't open | *Ez ezazu ireki* |

> This form is seen more in writing, on signs, and in a formal register. In daily speech, *ez hartu*, *ez ahaztu*, and *ez ireki* are perfectly valid and much more common.

## Giving advice: *hobe duzu / komeni zaizu*

For A2, here are two formulas that are good to memorize as a block:

### *Hobe duzu + -tzea* — "it's better that you..."

| Spanish | Basque |
|---|---|
| It's better that you rest | *Hobe duzu **deskantsatzea*** |
| It's better that you go early | *Hobe duzu goiz **joatea*** |
| It's better that you don't eat so much | *Hobe duzu hainbeste **ez jatea*** |

More distant: **hobe litzateke** + *-tzea* — "it would be better".

### *Komeni zaizu / komeni da* — "it's advisable for you / it's advisable"

| Spanish | Basque |
|---|---|
| It's advisable for you to walk more | *Gehiago **ibiltzea** komeni zaizu* |
| It's advisable to eat fruit | *Fruta **jatea** komeni da* |
| It's not advisable for you to smoke | *Erretzea **ez zaizu komeni*** |

### Other useful formulas

- *Garrantzitsua da + -tzea* — It's important…
- *Beharrezkoa da + -tzea* — It's necessary…
- *Saiatu + -tzen* — To try…
- *Onena da + -tzea* — The best thing is…

> *Onena da goizean goiz altxatzea.* — The best thing is to get up early in the morning.

## How to give a series of tips

Combine several formulas: a recommendation + a prohibition + an explanation.

> *Bizimodu osasuntsua eduki nahi duzu? **Hartu** fruta egunero. **Egin** kirola astean hiru aldiz. **Ez erre** eta **ez edan alkohol askorik**. Eta gauean, **saiatu** zazpi-zortzi ordu lo egiten. **Hobe duzu** lasai egotea, urduri egotea baino.*

## Mini-dialogue: at the nutritionist's office

> *— Olatz, gehiegi pisatzen duzu eta tentsioa altu daukazu. Aldatu egin behar duzu.*
>
> *— Zer egin behar dut, mediku?*
>
> *— **Hartu** barazkiak egunero. **Ez jan** azukrea. **Ez edan** edari gasdunik. **Saiatu** astean lau aldiz paseatzen, gutxienez ordubete. Eta **hobe duzu** lo asko egitea: **ez geratu** gauean berandu arte.*

## Prohibitions on signs

Typical phrases you'll see in bars, parks, and buses:

- *EZ ERRE* — No smoking
- *EZ UKITU* — Do not touch
- *ARRETA: ZAKURRA. EZ SARTU* — Warning: dog. Do not enter.
- *EZ OINEZ JOAN BIDEAREN ERDITIK* — Do not walk in the middle of the road.
- *MESEDEZ, EZ EGIN ZARATARIK* — Please, do not make noise.

> **Cultural note:** in many Basque towns, public signs are **bilingual** or only in Basque. Recognizing the most common prohibitions (*ez sartu, ez ukitu, ez erre, ez aparkatu*) helps you get around independently. It's a good exercise: when you walk through a Basque-speaking town, try to read all the signs before looking at the Spanish.
