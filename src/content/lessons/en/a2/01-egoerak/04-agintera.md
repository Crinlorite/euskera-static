The **imperative** (*agintera*) is used to give instructions, make requests, and give advice. In Basque, it's **much simpler than it seems**: the most common form almost always matches the verb's **participle**.

## The basic rule: use the participle

For many everyday commands, the verb appears in its participle form without an auxiliary:

| Spanish | Basque |
|---|---|
| Take | *Hartu* |
| Sit down | *Eseri* |
| Get up | *Jaiki* |
| Write | *Idatzi* |
| Read | *Irakurri* |
| Listen | *Entzun* |
| Repeat | *Errepikatu* |
| Wait | *Itxoin* |
| Close the door | *Itxi atea* |
| Open the window | *Ireki leihoa* |

> This is the **most common form** used in the classroom, on the street, and in written instructions. It works for both *zu* and *zuek*.

## Synthetic forms (trinkoak): *zatoz*, *zoaz*

Some very common verbs have specific synthetic forms for the imperative. These are the ones worth memorizing:

| Verb | You (zu, singular) | You (zuek, plural) |
|---|---|---|
| *etorri* (to come) | **Zatoz** | **Zatozte** |
| *joan* (to go) | **Zoaz** | **Zoazte** |
| *ekarri* (to bring) | **Ekarri** / **Ekar ezazu** | **Ekar ezazue** |
| *eraman* (to take/carry) | **Eraman** / **Eramazu** | **Eramazue** |
| *egon* (to be) | **Egon** / **Lasai** (take it easy!) | **Egon** |

> *— **Zatoz** hona, mesedez.*
> *— **Zoazte** lehenbailehen.*

## NOR-NORI-NORK imperative: *emadazu*, *esadazu*

When you give a command with a double object ("give me this", "tell him that"), Basque has very useful compact forms:

| Spanish | Basque |
|---|---|
| Give me | *Emadazu* / *Eman iezadazu* |
| Tell me | *Esadazu* / *Esan iezadazu* |
| Give him/her | *Emaiozu* / *Eman iezaiozu* |
| Tell him/her | *Esaiozu* / *Esan iezaiozu* |
| Give us | *Emaguzu* |
| Give me (plural you) | *Emadazue* |

These forms are long to learn; at A2 level, it's enough to know **emadazu / esadazu / emaiozu / esaiozu** and understand that they come from the verbs *eman* / *esan*.

## "Please" and softeners

The imperative in Basque **doesn't sound harsh** if you add a polite word:

- *Mesedez* — please
- *Ahal baduzu* — if you can
- *Faborez* (colloquial variant)
- *Eskerrik asko aldez aurretik* — thanks in advance

> *Emadazu ogia, **mesedez**.*
> *Itxoin pixka bat, **ahal baduzu**.*

## Instructions (recipes, signs, classroom)

The imperative is the standard form in:

- **Recipes:** *Moztu tipula. Bota olioa zartaginera. Frijitu bost minutuz.*
- **Signs:** *Itxi atea. Ez ukitu. Erabili maskara.*
- **Classroom:** *Ireki liburua. Idatzi koadernoan. Errepikatu nirekin.*
- **Doctor's office:** *Hartu pilula bat. Edan ur asko. Hartu atseden.*

## Mini-text

> *Sukaldari eskolan, irakasleak esaten du:*
>
> *— **Hartu** patata bat. **Garbitu** ondo. **Kendu** azala. **Moztu** zatitan eta **bota** ur irakinera. **Itxoin** hogei minutu. Eta **probatu** ondoren.*

> **Cultural note:** in Basque, the **bossy** tone of the imperative is softened by intonation and context, not so often with periphrasis ("could you..."). Accompanying the command with *mesedez* or a smile is usually enough. If you want something more polite, say *eman ahal didazu...?* (can you give me...?).
